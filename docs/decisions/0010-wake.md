# ADR 0010: Wake — the actuals-only visual primitive

*2026-05-22*

## Context

Onduler ships with two related radar visualizations: a generic 7-vertex breathing hex on the locked Anchors surface (designer-drawn, no relationship to user data), and the wedge-and-fuel-gauge radar on the unlocked Anchors surface (colored swell wedges, fuel-gauge fills, drag-handle targets, an actuals polygon underneath the scaffolding).

The two visuals don't share a primitive. The locked hex is generic; the unlocked radar is target-comparing. There's no surface that shows the user's real shape this week, stripped of targets and color-coded scaffolding — just the form their motions traced.

This matters for three reasons. The locked-page hex has no relationship to the user's behavior, so the locked surface feels static even as the user logs. The marketing direction (`docs/launch-plan.md`) wants a single signature visual that's mysterious and uniquely user-shaped — a shape no other product produces and no two users share. And the existing radar's actuals polygon is conceptually beautiful but visually buried under wedges and handles; nobody's life ever sees its own outline.

## Decision

A new primitive — the **wake** — extracted from the existing radar's actuals polygon, stripped of all scaffolding, rendered monochrome, and made the hero visual of the locked Anchors surface across every cadence. Live data, every cadence, from day one.

### What it is

The wake is the user's actuals shoulder polygon, drawn over a cycle window, with the colored swell wedges removed, the target handles removed, the drag affordance removed (locked surface; the unlocked Anchors radar still owns tuning), the polygon stroked monochrome (single near-white stroke on the deep-ocean ground, ~5% alpha interior fill), N vertex count where N equals the user's swell count (3-swell user has a wedge-shaped polygon; 10-swell user has a near-circular polygon), and a breathing animation matching the current locked hex (4–5s ease-in-out, scale 0.95–1.06, opacity 0.78–1.0).

### Geometry

Reuses the shoulder-polygon math already in `lib/radar.ts` (3N-vertex perimeter with chord-clamped dives between unequal neighbors). The actuals polygon traces each swell's wedge independently; the dives along boundary radials hold so a heavy swell never visually swallows a light neighbor. This is the fix shipped 2026-05-21 (radar polish batch) and is load-bearing for this ADR — the wake reuses the same `actualPolygonPath` and `shoulderVertex` helpers, with the wedge backdrop and target handles omitted from the rendering layer.

### Live data on every cadence

Every locked Anchors cadence (week / month / quarter / year) renders a live wake from the user's actuals over that cadence's window. An earlier proposal — random shapes on the locked-week page until the cadence unlocks, real shapes on the longer-cadence locked tiles — was rejected mid-design. The mystery is stronger when motions visibly impact the shape from day one ("wtf is this thing reacting to me?"), and the blank-slate edge case is handled by the coalesce-from-circle pattern below, not by a generic placeholder.

| Cadence | Window | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Week (locked or unlocked) | Sunday-anchored current week | `logs` filtered to active chapter + week |
| Month (locked) | Calendar month, 1st → today | `logs` filtered to active chapter + month |
| Quarter (locked) | Calendar quarter, Jan-Mar etc. | `logs` filtered to active chapter + quarter |
| Year (locked) | Calendar year, Jan 1 → today | `logs` filtered to active chapter + year |

Targets scale for non-weekly cadences via the existing `monthlyTargetDisplay` / `lifetimeTargetDisplay` helpers, but the wake itself doesn't render targets — only actuals.

### Coalesce-from-circle (the blank-slate state)

A brand-new user with zero logs sees a pulsing monochrome circle. As they log their first motion, the wake transitions from circle to N-gon by progressively pushing each axis vertex outward from the center along its radial. Every motion adds to the relevant swell axis; the perimeter responds.

This is where "every motion has an impact" stops being a tagline and starts being the literal behavior of the visual. The user logs once and sees the wake bulge along that swell's axis. There is no surface in Onduler that gives more immediate feedback on a single action.

Mechanically: each axis vertex's radial distance smoothly interpolates from its previous frame value to its current actual (~400ms ease); the center stays fixed; the breathing animation runs over the top (compose transforms); when all axes are at zero, the polygon math collapses to a circle of radius = blank-slate baseline (taste in implementation, ~34 visual units in the design mock).

### Wave-mode behavior (hide / reappear)

When a user enters wave mode, the wake disappears entirely and is replaced by the `WaveField` ocean canvas — there's no rhythm to trace, so there's no shape. The moment the user logs a motion during the wave, the wake reappears starting from a fresh pulsing circle, accumulating from whatever they log inside the wave.

This is the hybrid hybrid (hide + reappear): hidden by default during a wave, but the visual is never absent if the user is showing up. The wake answers presence, not absence.

On welcome-back from a wave (the existing `/wave/return/welcome` screen), the visual resets to a fresh pulsing circle and pairs with the "every motion has an impact" copy line. The user re-enters Tide mode and watches their wake form again.

### Vocabulary

User-facing term: **wake**. As in *your wake this week*, *the wake forms as you log*, *show me your wake* (in pair with the visual). The funeral-wake reading exists but is dominated by context everywhere the word appears in product.

The locked surf vocabulary expands from six terms (Tide / Wave / Swell / Motion / Waypoint / Anchor) to seven with Wake added. Sits cleanly inside the water family — a wake is what something in motion leaves behind, philosophically resonant with Onduler's "every motion has an impact" posture, nautical without being surf jargon.

No internal/external split needed for this primitive. The wake is a derived visualization, not a stored entity. No table renames, no code identifier sprawl. Helper functions take their pragmatic names (`getActualPolygon`, `<Wake>` component) without competing with the user-facing word.

### Marketing-side seeded random generator

For surfaces without a real user — postcards, landing-page hero, Instagram posts — a `generateRandomWake(seed, n)` helper produces a plausible wake with random N in [3, 10] and random per-axis actual values drawn from a tunable distribution (uniform [0.2, 1.0] of a baseline radius for now; bias if printed wakes look too noisy or too uniform). Deterministic from the seed so a given printed postcard always renders the same shape. Exported as standalone SVG for print and as a React component for the web landing page.

Lives in `lib/wakes.ts` (new). Reuses the same shoulder polygon path generator as the in-product wake, just fed synthetic actuals. The two callers (in-product real, marketing-side seeded) are siblings, not parent/child.

### Copy primitives

Three lines, each with a defined home:

| Line | Where it lives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **"Every motion leaves a wake."** | Postcard back; landing page above email field | Primary tagline. Teaches the cause-and-effect of the visual. Brand voice externally. |
| **"Show me your wake."** | Instagram post caption; landing-page hero image pair; postcard back | Pairs with the visual. The vulnerability lands because the image gives context. Never standalone. |
| **"Every motion has an impact."** | Welcome-back screen, paired with fresh pulsing circle | In-product. Mirror of the tagline in warmer phrasing for the wave-return moment. |

Rejected: *"What's in your wake?"* — identical cadence to *"What's in your wallet?"* (Capital One, running since 2000). Subconscious rhythm match undercuts the secret-club aesthetic. Off the list.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

**Random shapes on the locked-week page, real shapes on the longer cadences.** Rejected. The mystery argument cuts the other way — a generic placeholder on the most-frequently-visited locked surface (week) trains the user to ignore the visual. Live data from day one makes the visual responsive from the very first log, which is what builds the *what is this thing reacting to me* mystery the design is reaching for.

**Use the wake on the unlocked Anchors surface too, replacing the wedge radar.** Considered. Rejected for now: the unlocked Anchors radar is the tuning surface and needs the wedges + drag handles to do its job. The wake is a recognition surface, not a tuning surface. May revisit if the unlocked surface ever splits ("review" vs "tune" modes).

**Color the wake by swell.** Rejected explicitly. Monochrome is the point — the form of your life isn't fragmented by category, it's a single thing. Colored vertices would make the visual about *which swells are active*; monochrome makes it about the form.

**Call the primitive a "shape" or a "tide."** Rejected. *Shape* was already in user-facing use for the build picker (see ADR 0011 for the deflation that frees the word for geometric description in design conversations, not as the wake's name). *Tide* was already locked as the default daily mode — calling the visual a tide would collide on every existing surface.

## Implications

- **PROJECT.md**: vocabulary table gains Wake. Working agreements pick up a wake-section note (live data on locked pages, coalesce-from-circle on blank slate, hide-and-reappear in wave mode). Roadmap reorders to place the Wake + Starter sets session ahead of the haptics / Three Whys items currently sitting at top.
- **Next code session**: pre-tester checklist (haptics honesty, in-app feedback affordance, install instructions page, Sentry wiring) — does not depend on the wake. **Session after that**: the wake itself, paired with the Starter sets deflation (ADR 0011) since both touch the locked Anchors page and the welcome-back screen.
- **Implementation surface**: new `lib/wakes.ts` (in-product polygon helpers + marketing-side seeded random generator), new `<Wake>` component for the locked Anchors cadence tiles, modifications to `LockedPage.tsx` to swap the breathing hex for a live wake bound to the active user + cadence, wiring for the coalesce-from-circle animation on first-log and wave-return, hide-and-reappear behavior bound to `wave_checkins`.
- **`WaveField` extension**: the layered ocean canvas already on the weekly locked page extends to the month/quarter/year locked tiles in the same session — currently those tiles have plainer treatment, and the consistent backdrop is what makes the cadence stack read as a family.
- **Database**: no schema changes. Wakes are derived from existing `logs` and `motion_swells` data; nothing new persists.
- **Marketing**: `generateRandomWake` blocks the postcard design work and the landing-page hero. Session 3 of the launch plan depends on this.

## Origin

Decision reached in a Cowork strategy session on 2026-05-22. The conceptual seed was Josh's instinct that the locked-hex visual should derive from the user's actual logs — *the real shape of your life* — to build mystery via responsiveness. The vocabulary debate (shape / tide / wake) ran through several rounds; **wake** landed because it extends the water-family vocabulary without colliding with the existing six terms and carries the philosophical layer ("what you leave behind by being in motion") that matches Onduler's posture. The geometry reuse means the radar polish shipped 2026-05-21 (chord-clamped shoulder vertex preventing heavy swells from swallowing light neighbors) is exactly what makes the wake legible at small N.
